 **Pedagogy** is the "Philosophy of Teaching" (the *strategy*), while **Lesson Creation** is the "Instructional Design" (the *tactics*).

If your Lesson Creator agent is the **Architect** (building the rooms, placing the doors), the Pedagogy Agent is the **Interior Designer/User Experience Specialist** (deciding if the house should be a quiet library, a collaborative workshop, or a high-energy gym).

Here are the primary pedagogical methodologies you should know, and how they change the "rules" for your agent:

---

### 1. Direct Instruction (The "I Do, We Do, You Do" Model)

This is the most traditional form of teaching. It is highly structured and teacher-led.

* **The Strategy:** The teacher explains a concept, demonstrates it, performs it with the student, and then lets the student try alone.
* **Best for:** Foundational skills, math formulas, or learning specific software.
* **Agent Rule:** "Provide a step-by-step demonstration before asking the student to perform a task."

### 2. Inquiry-Based Learning (The "Scientist" Model)

Instead of giving answers, the teacher presents a "mystery" or a question.

* **The Strategy:** Students arrive at the facts by investigating. The teacher acts as a "guide on the side," not a "sage on the stage."
* **Best for:** Science, history, and social studies.
* **Agent Rule:** "Start with a complex phenomenon or data set. Ask the student to identify patterns before you explain the theory."

### 3. Problem-Based Learning (PBL)

This centers the entire lesson around a single, complex, real-world problem that has no single "correct" answer.

* **The Strategy:** Students learn the curriculum *as a byproduct* of trying to solve the problem.
* **Best for:** Engineering, business, and medicine.
* **Agent Rule:** "The lesson must be framed as a mission. Every piece of information provided must be framed as a 'tool' to help solve the specific mission."

### 4. Constructivism

Based on the idea that learners "construct" their own understanding based on previous experiences.

* **The Strategy:** The teacher focuses on the student's personal interpretation and encourages them to explain *their* version of the truth.
* **Best for:** Literature, philosophy, and creative arts.
* **Agent Rule:** "Frequently ask: 'How does this connect to your own life?' or 'How would you explain this in your own unique vocabulary?'"

### 5. The Flipped Classroom

This reverses the traditional flow of education.

* **The Strategy:** Students "consume" the content (reading/videos) *before* the session, and the "lesson" is entirely dedicated to practice and debate.
* **Best for:** Complex subjects where students often get "stuck" during homework.
* **Agent Rule:** "Keep the 'teaching' portion extremely concise (5 mins) and dedicate 90% of the interaction to troubleshooting and advanced application."

---

### Comparison: Why you need two different agents

To make your system powerful, the **Pedagogy Agent** should act as the "Supervisor" for the **Lesson Creator Agent**.

| Feature | Pedagogy Agent (The Strategy) | Lesson Creator Agent (The Content) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Question** | "How should the student feel and interact?" | "What content needs to be covered?" |
| **Output** | "Use a PBL approach with a focus on Inquiry." | "Here is the text, the quiz, and the summary." |
| **Focus** | Student psychology and engagement style. | Accuracy of information and structure. |

### How to use them together:

1. **User:** "I want a lesson on Photosynthesis."
2. **Pedagogy Agent:** "Since this is a science topic, I suggest **Inquiry-Based Learning**. I will instruct the Creator to start with a mystery: 'Why do plants die in the dark even if they have water?'"
3. **Lesson Creator Agent:** Receives the instruction and builds the modules, the hook, and the assessments based on that specific inquiry-based strategy.
